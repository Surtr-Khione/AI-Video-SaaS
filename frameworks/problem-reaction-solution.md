# Problem-Reaction-Solution (PRS) Framework

## Overview
The Problem-Reaction-Solution framework is a powerful storytelling structure that creates emotional engagement by first establishing a relatable problem, triggering an emotional response, and then presenting your product as the ideal solution.

## Structure

### 1. Problem (15-25% of video)
**Goal:** Establish the pain point that your target audience experiences daily.

**Key Elements:**
- Open with a specific, relatable scenario
- Use concrete examples, not abstractions
- Show the frustration/cost/impact of the problem
- Make viewers nod their heads in recognition

**Example Opening Lines:**
- "Imagine spending 3 hours every day switching between 12 different tools..."
- "Your team misses another deadline because someone forgot to update the spreadsheet..."
- "You've just lost a $50K deal because your proposal took too long to create..."

**Visual Direction:**
- Chaotic, cluttered scenes
- Frustrated characters or users
- Red/orange color grading for tension
- Quick cuts to show overwhelm

### 2. Reaction (10-15% of video)
**Goal:** Amplify the emotional impact and show the consequences.

**Key Elements:**
- Demonstrate the ripple effects
- Show what happens if the problem isn't solved
- Build urgency
- Connect to bigger business/personal impacts

**Example Reaction Elements:**
- "This isn't just frustrating—it's costing you 15 hours per week"
- "Your team's productivity has dropped 40%"
- "Meanwhile, competitors are moving faster..."
- Statistics and data that shock

**Visual Direction:**
- Time-lapse showing wasted time
- Dashboard metrics going down
- Worried faces in meetings
- Darker, more serious tone

### 3. Solution (60-70% of video)
**Goal:** Present your product as the natural, inevitable answer.

**Key Elements:**
- Transition from dark to light (literally in visuals)
- Show the "aha moment" of discovery
- Demonstrate key features solving specific problems
- Show the transformation and results
- End with clear call-to-action

**Subsections:**
- **Introduction (5%):** "There's a better way..."
- **Demonstration (40%):** Show features in action
- **Transformation (15%):** Before vs. After comparison
- **Call-to-Action (10%):** Clear next steps

**Visual Direction:**
- Brighter, cleaner aesthetic
- Smooth, confident interactions
- Blue/green color grading for calm
- Satisfied users
- Success metrics rising

## Timing Template

### 30-Second Version
- Problem: 8s
- Reaction: 4s
- Solution: 18s

### 60-Second Version
- Problem: 15s
- Reaction: 8s
- Solution: 37s

### 90-Second Version
- Problem: 20s
- Reaction: 15s
- Solution: 55s

## Best Used For
- ✅ B2B SaaS with clear pain points
- ✅ Productivity tools
- ✅ Enterprise software
- ✅ Tools that replace manual processes
- ✅ Solutions to widespread frustrations

## Real-World Examples
- **Slack:** "Email is overwhelming → Teams are disconnected → Slack brings it all together"
- **Notion:** "Too many tools → Information scattered → One workspace for everything"
- **Zapier:** "Manual data entry wastes time → Errors and delays pile up → Automate it all"

## Pro Tips
1. **Be Specific:** Don't say "teams are inefficient"—say "your designers wait 2 days for feedback"
2. **Use Numbers:** Quantify the problem and solution
3. **Show, Don't Tell:** Use screen recordings and real scenarios
4. **Emotional Arc:** Start negative, end positive—the contrast is powerful
5. **Authentic Pain:** Research real user frustrations; don't make them up

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- ❌ Making up problems that don't exist
- ❌ Spending too long on the problem (viewers get impatient)
- ❌ Not connecting features to the original problem
- ❌ Weak transition between sections
- ❌ Forgetting the CTA

## Variations

### **Aggressive PRS**
- Heavy emphasis on problem
- Dramatic reactions
- Use for: Disrupting established markets

### **Empathetic PRS**
- Softer problem presentation
- Understanding tone
- Use for: Sensitive topics, healthcare, personal tools

### **Data-Driven PRS**
- Statistics-heavy problem
- ROI-focused solution
- Use for: Enterprise sales, CFO/CEO audience
