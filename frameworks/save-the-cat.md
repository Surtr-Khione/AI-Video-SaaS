# Save the Cat Framework (Adapted for Product Demos)

## Overview
"Save the Cat" is Blake Snyder's legendary screenplay structure, adapted here for product demos. It creates emotionally engaging narratives by following the hero's journey structure—where your customer is the hero and your product is their guide/tool to transformation.

## The 15 Beats (Adapted for 60-120s Demos)

### 1. Opening Image (0-5s)
**Goal:** Establish the current state—the "before" snapshot.

**Key Elements:**
- Show the customer's world before your product
- Visual metaphor for their situation
- Set tone and aesthetic
- Create intrigue

**Examples:**
- Overwhelmed founder at desk with mountains of paperwork
- Developer frustrated by endless bug tickets
- Marketer staring at disappointing analytics
- Team scattered across multiple video calls

**Visual Direction:**
- Relatable, authentic situations
- Slightly desaturated or cooler tones
- Environmental establishing shot

---

### 2. Theme Stated (5-8s)
**Goal:** Hint at the lesson/transformation to come.

**Key Elements:**
- A question or statement about what matters
- Foreshadows the solution
- Philosophical hook

**Examples:**
- "What if work didn't have to feel like this?"
- "Great teams shouldn't struggle with bad tools"
- "Your ideas deserve better than limitations"
- "Speed shouldn't come at the cost of quality"

**Visual Direction:**
- Thoughtful pause
- Key visual symbol
- Typography emphasis on key words

---

### 3. Set-Up (8-20s)
**Goal:** Explore the status quo and establish what's at stake.

**Key Elements:**
- Show the daily routine/workflow
- Introduce pain points organically
- Demonstrate what the hero stands to lose
- Build empathy

**Examples:**
- Walk through a typical day with current tools
- Show the workarounds they've created
- Display the inefficiencies
- Reveal the costs (time, money, stress)

**Visual Direction:**
- Documentary-style realism
- Show actual workflows
- Multiple small frustrations
- Building tension

---

### 4. Catalyst (20-25s)
**Goal:** The moment of discovery—something must change.

**Key Elements:**
- "Enough is enough" moment
- Discovery of your product
- A specific trigger event
- Point of no return

**Examples:**
- Missing a crucial deadline
- Losing a major client
- Competitor launches first
- Team member quits due to frustration
- Someone mentions your product

**Visual Direction:**
- Dramatic shift in energy
- Visual/audio punctuation
- Close-up on realization
- Transition in music

---

### 5. Debate (25-30s)
**Goal:** Should they try this new solution? Acknowledge hesitation.

**Key Elements:**
- Brief moment of uncertainty
- Acknowledge common objections
- "Is this really different?"
- Creates authenticity

**Examples:**
- "Another tool? We've tried everything..."
- Looking at competitors/alternatives
- Checking reviews or asking colleagues
- Moment of skepticism

**Visual Direction:**
- Slower pacing
- Thoughtful expressions
- Comparing options
- Reading testimonials/reviews

---

### 6. Break Into Act Two (30-35s)
**Goal:** Decision to try your product—commitment to change.

**Key Elements:**
- Clear decision moment
- Sign up, start trial, or first use
- Crossing the threshold
- Music/visual shift to optimism

**Examples:**
- Clicking "Start Free Trial"
- First login screen
- Onboarding begins
- "Let's do this" moment

**Visual Direction:**
- Brighter, warmer tones
- Forward motion
- Confident action
- UI comes to life

---

### 7. B Story (35-40s)
**Goal:** Introduce the relationship/human element.

**Key Elements:**
- Show collaboration or team aspect
- Human connection to the product
- Not just solo use
- Community or support

**Examples:**
- Inviting team members
- Collaboration features in action
- Support team helping
- Community or integrations

**Visual Direction:**
- Multiple people interacting
- Friendly, warm tones
- Smiles and positive body language
- Connection visualization

---

### 8. Fun and Games (40-60s)
**Goal:** The "promise of the premise"—show features in action!

**Key Elements:**
- Core features demonstrated
- Enjoyment of using the product
- Things working smoothly
- Multiple "wow" moments
- This is the heart of most demos

**Examples:**
- Rapid feature showcase
- Smooth workflows
- Impressive automations
- Delightful interactions
- Time-saving in action

**Visual Direction:**
- Energetic pacing
- Satisfying animations
- Clear UI demonstrations
- Multiple quick wins
- Upbeat music peak

---

### 9. Midpoint (60-65s)
**Goal:** False victory or stakes raised—everything seems perfect.

**Key Elements:**
- Celebration of initial success
- "This is amazing!" moment
- But hint that there's more...
- Raising the bar

**Examples:**
- First big win with the product
- Impressive results dashboard
- Team celebrating
- "And it gets better..."

**Visual Direction:**
- Brief celebration
- Success metrics
- Triumphant moment
- Energy peak

---

### 10. Bad Guys Close In (65-70s)
**Goal:** Optional for demos—acknowledge challenges or limitations of other tools.

**Key Elements:**
- Quick reminder of alternative (worse) reality
- What if they hadn't made the change?
- Competitors still struggling
- Time-sensitive opportunity

**Examples:**
- "While others still struggle with [old way]..."
- Flash to alternative scenario
- Competitive comparison
- Market pressure

**Visual Direction:**
- Brief return to darker tones
- Split screen: them vs others
- Urgency building

---

### 11. All Is Lost (70-75s)
**Goal:** Optional for demos—the last objection or concern addressed.

**Key Elements:**
- Final potential barrier
- "But wait, what about...?"
- Common deal-breaker concern
- Setup for final transformation

**Examples:**
- "What about security?"
- "Can it scale?"
- "Will my team actually use it?"
- Price concern

**Visual Direction:**
- Brief pause
- Serious tone
- Direct address

---

### 12. Dark Night of the Soul (75-78s)
**Goal:** Moment of reflection before final commitment.

**Key Elements:**
- Acknowledgment of the decision weight
- Emotional authenticity
- "Is this the right choice?"

**Examples:**
- Customer testimonial moment
- Trusted advisor endorsement
- Data/proof point
- Moment of truth

**Visual Direction:**
- Intimate, honest moment
- Real customer faces
- Authentic testimonial
- Eye contact with camera

---

### 13. Break Into Act Three (78-85s)
**Goal:** Final commitment and full adoption.

**Key Elements:**
- Full integration into workflow
- Complete transformation
- All-in adoption
- New normal established

**Examples:**
- Entire team using it
- Integrated into daily routine
- Advanced features adopted
- Workflow optimized

**Visual Direction:**
- Smooth, flowing sequences
- Confident use
- Seamless integration
- Natural part of day

---

### 14. Finale (85-100s)
**Goal:** Show the complete transformation and results.

**Key Elements:**
- Before vs after comparison
- Tangible results and metrics
- Success story
- Victory achieved

**Examples:**
- Revenue up, costs down
- Time saved, quality improved
- Team happy, customers satisfied
- Goals achieved

**Visual Direction:**
- Side-by-side comparisons
- Rising metrics and charts
- Happy teams and customers
- Success montage
- Return to opening image location (but transformed)

---

### 15. Final Image (100-120s)
**Goal:** Mirror of opening image, but transformed.

**Key Elements:**
- Return to opening scene/location
- Show how everything has changed
- Emotional completion
- Clear call-to-action

**Examples:**
- Same founder at desk, but calm and smiling
- Same developer, now shipping features confidently
- Same marketer, now celebrating results
- Same team, now collaborating seamlessly

**Visual Direction:**
- Mirror opening composition
- Warmer, brighter tones
- Satisfied, confident energy
- End card with CTA

## Timing Templates

### 30-Second Version (Condensed)
- Opening Image: 2s
- Catalyst: 3s
- Break Into Two: 2s
- Fun and Games: 15s
- Finale: 5s
- Final Image + CTA: 3s

### 60-Second Version (Standard)
- Opening Image: 4s
- Set-Up: 8s
- Catalyst: 5s
- Break Into Two: 5s
- Fun and Games: 25s
- Finale: 10s
- Final Image + CTA: 3s

### 90-Second Version (Extended)
- Opening Image: 5s
- Theme: 3s
- Set-Up: 12s
- Catalyst: 6s
- Debate: 4s
- Break Into Two: 5s
- Fun and Games: 30s
- Midpoint: 5s
- Finale: 15s
- Final Image + CTA: 5s

### 120-Second Version (Full)
Use all 15 beats as outlined above

## Character Archetypes for Product Demos

### The Hero: Your Customer
- Has a goal or desire
- Faces obstacles
- Needs transformation
- Takes action

### The Guide: Your Product (and Brand)
- Has been there before
- Offers wisdom and tools
- Empowers the hero
- Doesn't steal spotlight

### The Villain: The Problem/Old Way
- Creates obstacles
- Represents status quo
- Source of frustration
- Must be defeated

### The Mentor: Testimonials/Support
- Validates the journey
- Offers reassurance
- Provides social proof
- Gives confidence

## Pro Tips

1. **Customer Is Hero:** Never make your product the hero—customer achieves the victory
2. **Emotional Beats Matter:** Don't skip the emotional moments for more features
3. **Visual Bookends:** Opening and closing images should mirror each other
4. **Music Follows Arc:** Score should match emotional journey
5. **Authentic Struggle:** Don't minimize the problem or rush through pain points
6. **Real Transformation:** Show measurable, believable change
7. **Universal Themes:** Tap into universal human desires (mastery, belonging, freedom)

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- ❌ Product as hero instead of customer
- ❌ Skipping emotional beats for features
- ❌ No clear transformation
- ❌ Weak opening image
- ❌ Forgetting the "save the cat" moment (making hero likeable)
- ❌ Too much time in pain, not enough in victory
- ❌ Ignoring the B-story (human connection)
- ❌ No visual callback in final image

## The "Save the Cat" Moment

In screenwriting, this is when the hero does something that makes us like them. In product demos:

**Make Your Customer Likeable:**
- Show them caring about their team
- Demonstrate their dedication
- Reveal their values
- Show vulnerability and authenticity
- Display competence despite obstacles

**Examples:**
- Founder working late to serve customers
- Manager protecting team's time
- Designer caring about user experience
- Developer debugging to help end users

## Variations

### **Hero's Journey Demo**
- Emphasis on transformation
- Multiple challenges and victories
- Longer form (2-3 minutes)
- Use for: Brand videos, annual recaps

### **Compressed Cat**
- Keep emotional beats, reduce feature time
- 30-45 seconds
- Use for: Social media, ads

### **Customer-First Cat**
- Real customer as hero throughout
- Actual footage/testimonial
- Use for: Case studies, testimonials

### **Founder Story Cat**
- Founder's journey to create the product
- Why it exists
- Use for: Mission-driven marketing, fundraising

---

## Integration with Other Frameworks

**Save the Cat + PRS:**
- Opening Image = Problem
- Catalyst = Reaction
- Fun & Games = Solution

**Save the Cat + FBTD:**
- Use FBTD structure within "Fun & Games" section
- Each feature gets its arc

**Save the Cat + AIDA:**
- Opening/Theme = Attention
- Set-Up/Catalyst = Interest
- Fun & Games = Desire
- Finale/Final = Action

## Real-World Adaptations

### **Slack: "So Yeah, We Tried Slack"**
- Opening: Chaotic office communication
- Catalyst: Too many missed messages
- Fun & Games: Slack channels organizing chaos
- Final: Team in sync, laughing together

### **Airbnb: "Belong Anywhere"**
- Opening: Lonely traveler in sterile hotel
- Catalyst: Desire for authentic connection
- Fun & Games: Staying in unique Airbnb homes
- Final: Traveler feels at home anywhere

### **Apple: Product Launches**
- Opening: Limitations of current tech
- Theme: "What if we could..."
- Fun & Games: Feature showcase
- Final: World transformed by new product
